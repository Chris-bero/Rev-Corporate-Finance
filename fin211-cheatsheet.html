1 · Foundations & Key Terms Lec 1 · Lec 2-P1top

Finance
Management of money by individuals (personal), corporations (corporate), governments (public).
Corporate finance
Deals with capital structure (debt + equity); maximizes shareholders' wealth via short- & long-term financing plans.
Corporate goal
Maximize shareholder wealth (share price), not merely profit.
Capital management
Provide short-term liquidity through debt & equity. Too much debt → default risk; too much equity → lower EPS.
Investment
Purchase of assets (stocks, bonds, real estate…) to earn dividends, interest, capital gains.
Capital budgeting
Decision process for analyzing potential investments in fixed assets.
Budgeting
Planning money by estimating future income/expenses over a period.
Financial markets
Any marketplace where securities trade (stock, bond, forex, derivatives).
Financial services
Design & delivery of advice and financial products to individuals, firms, governments.
Areas of corporate finance
Capital budgeting · capital financing · working-capital management.

Five principles of finance

  • Time value of money — a dollar today > a dollar tomorrow.
  • Risk–return trade-off — higher risk ⇒ higher return.
  • Cash flows are the source of value (cash that can be taken out of the business).
  • Market prices reflect information (buy & sell ⇒ equilibrium).
  • Individuals respond to incentives (motivation).

Accounting vs Finance

  • Focus: accounting = past & present · finance = future-oriented decisions.
  • Basis: accounting = accrual (IFRS/GAAP) · finance = cash (risk, TVM, value).

Earnings per share

EPS = Net income − Preferred dividendsOutstanding common shares
Example — EPS NI 1,000,000; pref. div. 100,000; 450,000 shares → EPS = 900,000/450,000 = $2.00. Bakery: (20,000−2,000)/10,000 = 1.8 (slides round to ≈2.0). More shares issued ⇒ lower EPS.

2 · Cash Flow, OCF, FCF & FCFF Lec 2-P2 · Lec 3top

Cash-flow categories

  • Operating: cash sales, collecting A/R, tax refunds | paying wages, payables, overhead, interest.
  • Investing: sale / purchase of fixed assets.
  • Financing: sale of stock, borrowing | buying back stock, dividends, debt repayment.

Depreciation (MACRS, 5-year recovery)

Rates: Yr1 20% · Yr2 32% · Yr3 19% · Yr4 12% · Yr5 12% · Yr6 5%  →  Dept = Installed cost × ratet
Example — $100,000 machine Depreciation: 20,000 · 32,000 · 19,000 · 12,000 · 12,000 · 5,000 (total 100,000). Depreciation matters: non-cash charge + tax shield.

Operating cash flow

NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − T)   |   OCF (unlevered) = EBIT × (1 − T) + Depreciation
OCF (levered) = Net income after tax + Depreciation

Unlevered = cash to all capital providers before debt & equity; levered = after interest/debt servicing.

Example — Baker Corp. OCF = 370 × (1 − .40) + 100 = $322 (thousands).

Free cash flow (FCF)

FCF = OCF − NFAI − NCAI
NFAI = Δ Net fixed assets + Depreciation   |   NCAI = Δ Current assets − Δ (A/P + Accruals)
Example — Baker Corp. NFAI = (1,200 − 1,000) + 100 = 300 · NCAI = (2,000 − 1,900) − (800 − 700) = 0 · FCF = 322 − 300 − 0 = $22.

Free cash flow to the firm (FCFF) ★

FCFF = NI + Depreciation + [Interest × (1 − T)] − Fixed-asset investment − Δ Working capital

FCFF = cash available to debt holders, equity holders and re-investment.

Example 1 NI 500, D&A 120, Int 100, T 30%, FA inv 200, WC inv 50 → FCFF = 500 + 120 + 100(0.70) − 200 − 50 = 440.
Example 2 — ABC Co. NI 245,000 + Dep 100,000 + Int 50,000(0.70) − FA inv (850,000−600,000) − ΔWC [(440,000−180,000) − (350,000−150,000)] = 245,000 + 100,000 + 35,000 − 250,000 − 60,000 = 70,000.

Cash-flow ratios

Cash-flow margin = OCFSales revenue   |   Cash-flow coverage = OCFTotal debt

Margin: high = strong cash conversion; negative = operations consume cash. Coverage: high = debt repaid quickly (>1 ⇒ under one year); 0.1–0.3 acceptable; <0.1 low; negative = financial risk. ABC: margin = 345,000/2,000,000 = 0.1725; coverage = 345,000/450,000 ≈ 0.77.

3 · Cash Budgeting Lec 4-P1top

Financial planning = cash planning (cash budget) + profit planning (pro-forma statements). The cash budget shows expected cash shortages / surpluses month by month.

The three steps

  • Step 1 — Cash receipts: cash sales (% of sales) + collections of A/R lagged 1 month and 2 months + other receipts.
  • Step 2 — Cash disbursements: purchases (% of sales) paid cash / lagged 1 mo / lagged 2 mo + rent, wages & salaries, taxes, fixed-asset outlays, interest, dividends, principal.
  • Step 3 — Financing position:
Net cash flow = Receipts − Disbursements  →  Ending cash = Net CF + Beginning cash
Ending < Minimum ⇒ Required total financing (notes payable)  |  Ending > Minimum ⇒ Excess cash (marketable securities)

5 · Stock Valuation Lec 8top

Expected return (dividends + capital gains)

E(r) = DP + g

D = dividend, P = price, g = growth. E.g. $1 dividend on $25 stock, g = 7% → 1/25 + 0.07 = 11%.

Zero-growth model (perpetuity; also preferred stock)

P0 = D1 × 1rs = D1rs
Examples Constant $3 dividend, rs = 15% → P0 = 3/0.15 = $20. Table: 3/0.12 = 25 · 5/0.10 = 50 · 4/0.16 = 25 · 2.5/0.08 = 31.25 · 6/0.15 = 40.

Constant-growth model (Gordon) ★

P0 = D1rs − g,   D1 = D0(1 + g)
Examples D0 = 1.40, g = 7%, rs = 15% → 1.40(1.07)/(0.15−0.07) = $18.75. Table: 2/(0.10−0.04) = 33.33 · 3/(0.12−0.05) = 42.86 · 1.5/(0.09−0.03) = 25 · 2.5/(0.11−0.06) = 50 · 4/(0.14−0.05) = 44.44.

Buy if value > price (undervalued)Sell/avoid if value < price (overvalued)

Must-know terms

  • Common stock: residual claimants; voting rights; returns = dividends + capital gains.
  • Share types: authorized · issued · outstanding · treasury; par vs no-par.
  • IPO: needs shareholder approval + investment bank underwriting (bank buys issue at agreed price, bears selling risk).
  • Preferred stock: fixed dividend, prior claim, cumulative (arrears), paid before common; hybrid of bond & stock.

6 · Risk, Return, Portfolio & CAPM Lec 8 · Lec 9top

Individual asset

E(R) = Σ Pi Ri   |   σ² = Σ Pi(Ri − E(R))²   |   σ = √σ²
Example — states of nature (P: recession .2, normal .5, boom .3) Stock C: E(R) = .2(.02)+.5(.10)+.3(.15) = 9.9%; σ² = 0.002029 → σ = 4.5%. Stock T: E(R) = 17.7%. Total risk = σ; systematic risk = β (C: σ20%/β1.25 vs K: σ30%/β0.95 → K more total risk, C more systematic risk & higher expected return).

Portfolio return & weights

wi = InvestmentiTotal portfolio (Σw = 1)   |   E(Rp) = Σ wi E(Ri)
Examples $15,000: IREX 2,000 (.1333, 19.65%) · AIND 3,000 (.2, 8.96%) · EFID 4,000 (.266, 9.67%) · EKHO 6,000 (.4, 8.13%) → E(Rp) = .0262+.0179+.0257+.0325 = 10%. AbuAuf & VCola — half the money in each, 14% return each → 14%.

Two-asset portfolio standard deviation ★

σp = √( w1²σ1² + w2²σ2² + 2 w1w2 ρ1,2 σ1σ2 )
Example — w .5/.5, σ .2/.2, ρ = +0.75 σp = √(.5²·.2² + .5²·.2² + 2·.5·.5·.75·.2·.2) = √0.035 = 0.187. ρ = +0.75 ⇒ returns move together but not in perfect unison.

Types of risk

  • Systematic (market / non-diversifiable): GDP, inflation, interest rates — measured by β.
  • Unsystematic (unique / asset-specific): strikes, part shortages — eliminated by diversification.
  • Total risk = σ; don't hold one asset or one industry.

Portfolio beta

βp = Σ wi βi

.133(3.69) + .2(0.64) + .167(1.64) + .4(1.79) = 1.61.

CAPM ★

Re = Rf + β (Rm − Rf)

Rf = risk-free rate (e.g. T-bills), Rm = market return, (Rm−Rf) = market risk premium. Higher β ⇒ higher risk premium.

Examples & decision rule Rf 4%, Rm 10%, β 1.5 → Re = 4 + 1.5(6) = 13%. Rf 3%, Rm 11% (premium 8%): A β.8 → 9.4% vs exp 8% ⇒ No; B β1.2 → 12.6% vs 14% ⇒ Yes; C β1.5 → 15% vs 16% ⇒ Yes. Rf 5%, Rm 12%: X β.5 → 8.5% vs 7% No; Y β1.0 → 12% vs 12% Yes; Z β1.8 → 17.6% vs 18% Yes.
Accept: Expected return ≥ CAPM returnReject: Expected return < CAPM return

7 · Time Value of Money & Bond Valuation Lec 10top

Simple interest

FV = PV × (1 + r·n)   |   PV = FV1 + r·n

PV 100 at 6% for 1 year → FV 106. FV table (PV, r, n → FV): (10,000, 5%, 3) → 11,500 · (25,000, 7%, 4) → 32,000 · (5,000, 6%, 1.5) → 5,450. PV table (FV, r, n → PV): (12,000, 5%, 3) → 10,434.78 · (20,000, 6%, 4) → 16,129.03 · (8,000, 9%, 2) → 6,779.66.

Compound interest

FV = PV × (1 + r)n   |   PV = FV(1 + r)n

PV 100 at 6% for 5 years → FV 133.8. FV table (PV, r, n → FV): (10,000, 5%, 3) → 11,576.25 · (15,000, 8%, 5) → 22,039.92 · (50,000, 6%, 10) → 89,542.4. PV table (FV, r, n → PV): (15,000, 5%, 4) → 12,340 · (30,000, 6%, 3) → 25,188 · (10,000, 8%, 10) → 4,631.93.

Annuities (end-of-year payments)

FV = PMT × (1 + r)n − 1r   |   PV = PMT × 1 − (1 + r)−nr

PMT = 5,000 paid at the end of each year for 5 years, at r = 5%: FV = 5,000 × 5.5256 = 27,628; PV = 5,000 × 4.32948 = 21,647.4.

Bond terms

  • Loans = private debt (two parties). Pros: speed · reduced costs · financing flexibility. Cons: interest cost higher than bonds · restrictive covenants · possible future SEC registration. Bonds = public debt, traded in bond markets.
  • Indenture = legal contract with trustee; bondholders' claims senior to common stock.
  • Par value = face repaid at maturity; coupon rate = % of par paid yearly.
  • Call provision = issuer may redeem early; conversion = bond → shares.
  • Floating rate = index (LIBOR) of prior period + spread, subject to cap & floor.
Current yield = Annual interestCurrent market price  |  Accrued interest = Months since payment12 × Annual coupon

8% coupon, $1,000 par, price $700 → 80/700 = 11.4%; 6 months accrued = 6/12 × 80 = $40. Floating loan (LIBOR+0.25, cap 2.5, floor 1.75): wk2 2.23 · wk3 1.91 · wk4 1.77 · wk5 1.75 (floor) · wk6 1.85 · wk7 1.88 · wk8 1.92 · wk9 2.13 · wk10 2.18.

Bond value ★

B0 = C × 1 − (1 + r)−nr + Par(1 + r)n

= PV of coupon annuity + PV of par. C = coupon rate × par.

Examples 12% required, $120 coupon, 5yr → 432.576 + 567.427 = 1,000 (par). 15% → 402.259 + 497.177 = 899.44 (discount). 9% → 466.758 + 649.93 = 1,116.68 (premium). Coupon 8% with required yield 11.4% over 10 years → 463.4 + 339.7 = 803.1. Table (coupon rate, required yield r, years → bond value): (7%, 5%, 3) → 1,054.47 · (8%, 10%, 4) → 936.60 · (6%, 7%, 5) → 959.00.
r > coupon ⇒ discount · r = coupon ⇒ par · r < coupon ⇒ premium.

Accept/buy: DCF value > market price (undervalued)Reject: DCF value < market price (overvalued)

8 · Capital Budgeting Lec 12top

Capital budgeting = decision process for analyzing potential fixed-asset investments. Important because of: growth, large amounts, irreversibility, complexity, risk, long-term implications. Uses: new product/service, replacement, expansion, R&D, long-term contracts, safety/environment.

Techniques

  • Non-discounted: Payback period (PBP) · Average rate of return (ARR).
  • Discounted: Discounted PBP · NPV · IRR · Profitability index.

Payback period

Constant inflow: PBP = Initial investmentAnnual fixed cash inflow
Variable inflow: PBP = Completed years + Remaining outflowCash inflow of year t
Examples Constant: A 50,000/12,000 = 4.17yr · B 60,000/15,000 = 4yr (best) · C 45,000/10,000 = 4.5yr.
Variable: A 50,000 → cum 15/30/40/46 + 4,000/5,000 = 4.8yr; B 60,000 → cum 20/40/50/56 + 4,000/6,000 = 4.66yr (best); C 45,000 → cum 12/24/36/42 + 3,000/4,000 = 4.75yr. Shorter PBP = better

Average rate of return

ARR = Average profit after taxAverage investment   or   Average profit after taxInitial investment

Determines the average annual earning of a project.

9 · International Financial Management Lec 13top

  • MNC: produces & sells in more than one country; parent in home country + typically 5–6 foreign subsidiaries with strategic interaction.
  • Classifications: international · multinational · transnational corporations.
  • Formation (FDI): buying an entire company, or partial ownership (≥10% of stock).
  • Motives: raw-material seekers (BP, Exxon, Total) · market seekers (per-capita markets) · cost minimizers (Microsoft).
  • Goal: maximize shareholder wealth (share price + coupons).
  • Roles: global manager (adjust to volatility) · politician & labor-union face (jobs/exports at home vs abroad).
  • Difficulties: exchange-rate risk · inflation risk · tax-rate differences · multiple financial markets · political risk.
  • FX market participants: bank & non-bank dealers · individuals & firms · speculators & arbitragers · central banks & treasuries · brokers.

Exchange-rate regimes

  • Hard pegs: gave up monetary sovereignty (adopt another currency / currency board).
  • Soft pegs: fixed exchange rates.
  • Floating: freely floating (market supply & demand, occasional intervention) · managed floating (secret targets, heavy intervention).
  • Residual: everything else.

Quotations

Direct = home-currency price of 1 unit foreign  |  Indirect = foreign-currency price of 1 unit home

European terms = indirect for Americans; American terms = direct. E.g. 22-Jan-2020: $1 = EGP 15.80 = 0.90 € = 2.82 TD.
